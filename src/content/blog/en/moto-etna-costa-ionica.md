Few areas of Italy offer, within a single day, a landscape contrast quite like the one between the hairpin bends of Etna's south side and the sea-cliff roads of the Ionian coast. For anyone travelling by motorbike, this combination is one of the most rewarding routes in eastern Sicily — and Pedara, home to Magma 610, is a convenient starting point for tackling it without having to move your luggage from one place to another.

## The route from Pedara to the coast

From Pedara, you climb quickly toward Etna's mid-altitude slopes, along scenic roads that cross high-altitude woods, historic lava flows and small towns like Nicolosi, Zafferana Etnea or Milo, depending on the side chosen. A full loop taking in several of the volcano's viewpoints takes roughly 2-3 hours of riding, not counting stops.

From there, descending toward the coast in the direction of Taormina or Giardini Naxos, the scenery changes completely: the roads become narrower and more winding, with sea views opening up at every bend. The coastal stretch between Taormina and Acireale, ridden along the old state road rather than the motorway, is probably the most photogenic point of the whole itinerary.

A full loop, Etna plus coast, comfortably fills a whole day: setting off from Pedara early in the morning, you can be back by late afternoon without rushing.

## What to see along the way

Climbing toward Etna, it's worth stopping in the small villages dotted along the south side — often overlooked by those who stick to the most touristy destinations — for a coffee in the square or to admire the local lava-stone architecture. The viewpoints toward the Silvestri Craters or Piano Provenzana offer brief but spectacular stops, with Etna dominating the landscape in every direction.

On the coast, **Taormina** deserves a stop even just for a short walk along Corso Umberto or a pause at the Piazza IX Aprile viewpoint, before getting back on the bike for the next leg. Those with more time can continue to Giardini Naxos or Capo Taormina, where the coastal roads offer sea views at every turn.

## Practical tips for riders

The best season for this route runs from April to October: in the colder months, Etna's mid- and high-altitude sections can have damp or icy stretches in the early morning, and temperatures at altitude drop quickly compared to the coast. Layered clothing under your riding gear helps manage the sharp temperature swing between the climb toward the volcano and the return to the sea.

Etna's high-altitude roads are scenic but often narrow, with tight hairpins and a surface that can be pitted or damaged by more recent lava flows: ride at a moderate speed and stay alert, especially in shaded stretches where the asphalt stays damp longer. On the coast, summer tourist traffic can slow things down in towns like Taormina: it's best to plan stops for the quieter hours, mid-morning or late afternoon.

Before setting off, it's worth filling up: petrol stations become scarcer as you climb, and not all stay open out of season.
