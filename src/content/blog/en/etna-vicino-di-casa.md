In Pedara, Etna isn't a destination you travel to — it's already there, visible from every courtyard and terrace at Magma 610. You wake up with the volcano's profile in front of you, and in the evening you watch it turn pink at sunset. For anyone staying on the south side, Europe's most active volcano isn't an excursion to plan weeks in advance: it's a next-door neighbour, a few minutes away by car.

This article is for anyone who has never set foot on Etna and wants to plan a first outing without surprises — from viewpoints you can drive to, to more demanding trails for those who want to head up toward the summit craters.

## The most accessible viewpoints

You don't need to be an experienced hiker to enjoy Etna. Several viewpoints on the south side are directly accessible by car, with parking and short approach walks:

- **Rifugio Sapienza**: the classic starting point, with views of the Silvestri craters and cable car access for those who want to go higher without walking too much.
- **Silvestri Craters**: easy loop trails, suitable for families with children too, winding among extinct volcanic cones and historic lava flows.
- **Piano Provenzana**, on the north side: less crowded, with a different, more wooded atmosphere — ideal for those seeking quiet.

## Planning a first hike

For those who want to go beyond the viewpoints and take on a real trail, a few practical tips:

The best period runs from April to October, when trails are generally free of snow and temperatures allow hiking without winter gear. In winter Etna is still open to visitors, but most higher-altitude routes require specific equipment (snowshoes, an expert guide).

For a first approach, we recommend booking with one of the many local mountain guides who organise guided excursions toward the summit craters — not just for safety (Etna remains an active volcano, with access zones regulated according to activity level), but also because a guide knows how to tell the geology and history of the place in a way that's easy to miss on your own.

## What to bring

- Hiking boots with a grippy sole: the lava terrain is sharp and unstable in several spots.
- Layered clothing: the temperature at altitude can be 15–20 degrees lower than in the valley, regardless of season.
- More water than usual: dry air and altitude dehydrate you faster.
- A windbreaker, even in high summer: wind at altitude is almost always present.

## The best time of day

Early morning generally offers the clearest views, before the afternoon haze typical of Sicilian summers reduces visibility toward the coast. Anyone wanting to catch sunrise from the summit craters should plan a very early start and, if not an experienced hiker, the company of a guide — the trail in the dark isn't recommended for those unfamiliar with the route.

From the south side, where Magma 610 is located, many of the starting points for these excursions are just 20–30 minutes away by car — one more reason we recommend guests set aside at least a full day for Etna during their stay, without rushing.
