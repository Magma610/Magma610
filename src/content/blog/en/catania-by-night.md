Catania changes face after sunset. The warm light illuminating the baroque palaces, the more relaxed pace compared to the middle of the day, air that in the summer months stays pleasant until late at night: the city at night has a different atmosphere from its daytime self, more lived-in and less tied to the rhythms of passing tourism. For guests staying at Magma 610, just 20-25 minutes by car, it's a convenient opportunity for an evening out of the ordinary.

## The squares after dark

Piazza del Duomo, with the illuminated Cathedral of Sant'Agata and Elephant Fountain, takes on a different atmosphere at night than during the day: the warm lighting brings out the baroque details of the cathedral's facade, and the square fills with locals stopping to chat, more than with organised tour groups. It's one of the best places to start an evening stroll, perhaps with a stop at one of the historic cafés overlooking the square.

Piazza Università, not far away, offers an even livelier atmosphere thanks to the constant presence of students: on warmer evenings, especially between spring and autumn, it becomes an informal meeting spot, with outdoor tables and a constant buzz that captures the city's younger, everyday side well.

## A walk along via Etnea

Via Etnea, the main street running through Catania for almost three kilometres, is perfect for an evening stroll: shops still open late in the warmer months, historic cafés and gelaterias to stop at for a dessert after dinner, and the view of Etna, which on the clearest nights can be glimpsed in the distance even after dark, when the volcano is active, with reddish glows visible from the city.

Walking along via Etnea in the evening, perhaps starting from Piazza del Duomo heading north, is a pleasant way to soak up the city's atmosphere with no fixed destination, letting yourself be guided by the lit-up shop windows and the flow of people along the side streets that cross the route.

## Practical tips

For evening parking, the areas near Piazza dei Martiri or along Corso Italia are generally easier to manage than during the day, with less traffic and more available spaces. It's still worth checking any time restrictions on limited-traffic zones, which in some streets of the historic centre remain active even in the evening on weekends.

For the return to Pedara, the route is the same as during the day — about 20-25 minutes by car — but with generally smoother traffic than during afternoon rush hour, making the drive back easy even after a long evening in the city.
