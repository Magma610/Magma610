Catania isn't just baroque and monuments. There's a louder, livelier city to discover among the markets, squares and street food stalls that keep the centre buzzing from morning until night — a side of Catania experienced more through smell and taste than through a camera lens. For guests staying at Magma 610, just 20-25 minutes by car, it's a trip well suited to a day built around food and street atmosphere.

## The Pescheria market

The Pescheria market, just steps from Piazza del Duomo, is the beating heart of the most authentic Catania: an open-air fish market where vendors call out to customers among stalls loaded with swordfish, tuna, prawns and the freshest seafood, just unloaded from the fishing boats. The atmosphere is intense, loud, at times chaotic — but that's exactly why it's worth visiting, even just to walk through without buying anything.

The best time to visit is early morning, between 7:30 and 9:30 am: that's when the market is in full swing, with the freshest goods on display and the most vendors around. By early afternoon the stalls are already starting to pack up, and the atmosphere loses much of its energy. Anyone wanting to try something on the spot will often find small stalls selling fresh sea urchin or raw seafood, a direct way to connect with Catanese food tradition.

## Catanese street food

Catania is one of the capitals of Sicilian street food, and walking through the centre without stopping to try something would be a missed opportunity. Among the most typical specialties to try are the arancino (strictly masculine here, unlike other parts of Sicily), often filled with ragù, butter or pistachio; the cipollina, a puff pastry filled with béchamel and ham; and scaccia, a kind of filled focaccia typical of the Ragusa area but now found across eastern Sicily.

Not to be missed, especially in the warmer months, is granita — a genuine daily ritual in Catania, often served with a brioche col tuppo for breakfast. Street stalls and rosticcerie scattered across the historic centre generally offer a good starting point for an informal taste, with no need to seek out a specific place: just follow the smell of fried food and the local crowd.

## The squares not to miss

Between one taste and the next, the historic squares remain the thread running through the day. Piazza del Duomo, dominated by the Cathedral of Sant'Agata and the Elephant Fountain, remains the central point of reference — handy even just as a starting and ending point for finding your way between the market and the surrounding streets.

Not far away, Piazza Università, with its eighteenth-century palaces and livelier atmosphere thanks to the constant presence of students, offers a pleasant contrast to the solemnity of Piazza del Duomo: historic cafés, benches to rest your legs after the walk through the market, and a good vantage point for observing everyday Catanese life away from the more touristy circuits.
