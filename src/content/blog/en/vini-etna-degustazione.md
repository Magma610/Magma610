Etna wines have, in recent years, earned a place among Italy's most talked-about appellations, and not by chance. Native grape varieties that find unique conditions on Etna, volcanic soil rich in minerals and different from one contrada to the next, and vineyards reaching up to 1000 metres in altitude: these are the elements that make the volcano's wines recognisable even to first-time tasters. For guests staying at Magma 610 who want to visit a winery on the south side, knowing a few basics beforehand makes the experience far more interesting.

## The main grape varieties

Etna's signature grape, for red wines, is **Nerello Mascalese**: it produces elegant wines, often compared for structure and ageing potential to the great wines of Burgundy, with delicate tannins and a marked minerality reflecting the volcanic soil. It's often blended with **Nerello Cappuccio**, used in small percentages to add colour and softness.

Among white wines, **Carricante** dominates, a grape that on Etna expresses one of its freshest, most vertical styles, with pronounced acidity and notes recalling citrus and mountain herbs — thanks largely to the altitude of the vineyards, which in the higher contrade helps keep temperatures lower even in high summer.

You don't need to be an expert to appreciate these differences: it's enough to know that, broadly speaking, "Etna red" almost always means Nerello Mascalese, while "Etna white" almost always means Carricante — a simple starting point for navigating the labels offered during a tasting.

## What to expect from a guided tasting

A typical winery tasting usually offers three or four labels, often in increasing order of body — starting with a lighter white or rosé, working up to a fuller-bodied red, perhaps barrel-aged. The average duration ranges from 45 minutes for a quick tasting up to two or three hours for the more complete tours, which include a walk among the vines and a visit to the historic cellar.

The pairings offered almost always follow local tradition: Sicilian cheeses, cured meats, extra virgin olive oil produced in the area, sometimes served with homemade bread. Some wineries also offer more elaborate pairings, with small dishes designed specifically to accompany each wine being tasted.

## Tips for first-time tasters

No specific preparation is needed to enjoy a tasting on Etna: most wineries are used to welcoming both seasoned enthusiasts and simply curious visitors, and staff are generally happy to take their time explaining the characteristics of each wine to those without much experience.

One useful tip is not to rush to form a judgement: the mineral character typical of Etna wines can come as a surprise to those used to softer, fruitier wines, and it often takes an extra sip or two to truly appreciate it. Always asking which contrada the grapes used for each label come from is a good way to start telling apart the different areas of the volcano — north, south, east side — which, despite being close to one another, produce wines with quite different characteristics.
