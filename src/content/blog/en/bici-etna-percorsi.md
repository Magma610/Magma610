Etna has long been a beloved destination for cycle touring, and not just for the most trained cyclists chasing demanding climbs. The south side, home to Magma 610, offers a range of routes from relaxed rides among vineyards and lava-stone walls to tougher ascents toward the volcano's higher altitudes — terrain that satisfies both those looking for a relaxed ride and those wanting to push themselves.

## Routes for every level

For those seeking an easy outing, the flat routes or gentle-gradient roads between the hillside towns of the south side — Trecastagni, Viagrande, Zafferana Etnea — are ideal: secondary roads, little traffic during the middle of the day, and scenery alternating between vineyards, citrus groves and Etna views at every bend. These routes are accessible even without specific training, with easy stops in the villages for a break or a coffee.

Those seeking a bigger challenge can head for the higher altitudes: the roads climbing toward Piano Vetore or Rifugio Sapienza have significant elevation gain, with gradients exceeding 10% in some stretches, and scenery that changes progressively — from vineyards to high-altitude woods, up to the barer lava fields near two thousand metres. These routes are reserved for those with climbing experience, ideally on a road or gravel bike suited to the surface, which becomes more uneven in places.

## The south side as a starting point

Starting from Pedara, home to Magma 610, means direct access both to the flat routes among the hillside towns and to the start of the high-altitude climbs, without having to transport your bike and gear elsewhere. The central location relative to the south side lets you choose a different route each day based on your form or the weather, without having to plan everything in advance.

For those staying several days, this means being able to alternate demanding rides with recovery days on lighter routes, all from the same base — no small advantage for anyone organising a cycling-focused stay.

## Practical tips

The best season for cycle touring on Etna runs from April to June and from September to October, when temperatures are milder and allow for climbs without the excessive heat of the Sicilian summer. In high summer, those who still want to ride would do well to set off very early in the morning, before the heat becomes demanding, especially on the more exposed stretches.

Some roads on the south side, especially those closest to the towns, can have heavy traffic at certain times of day: it's best to avoid peak hours and prefer, where possible, quieter secondary roads, which are often also more scenic.

For those not travelling with their own bike, the area has several rental points, offering road bikes, mountain bikes and e-bikes, convenient for anyone wanting to try a ride on Etna without the commitment of bringing their own.
