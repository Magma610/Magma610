Catania is the closest city to Magma 610, and also one of the most surprising in eastern Sicily. Rebuilt almost entirely in baroque style after the 1693 earthquake, with Etna's lava stone as the dominant building material, the city's historic centre has been recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage Site — a heritage that can comfortably be visited in a day, with no need to plan a separate stay.

## What to see

**Piazza del Duomo** is the heart of the city and the natural starting point for a visit: dominated by the Cathedral of Sant'Agata and the famous Elephant Fountain — u Liotru, Catania's symbol, carved from lava stone — the square packs, in just a few metres, the essence of Catanese baroque, with dark facades and white decorative details creating a contrast typical of the local architecture.

From here runs **via Etnea**, the city's main street, stretching for almost three kilometres straight toward Etna, visible in the background on clear days. It's the avenue lined with the city's most important noble palaces, historic shops and cafés where you can stop for a coffee or a granita, another Catanese specialty not to be missed.

The **Pescheria fish market**, just steps from Piazza del Duomo, is probably the most authentic experience the city has to offer: an open-air fish market, loud and lively, where vendors call out to customers among stalls piled with swordfish, tuna and the freshest seafood. Even just walking through it, without buying anything, is a way to understand Catania's more genuine side.

## How to get there from Pedara

Catania is about a 20-25 minute drive from Pedara, making it the quickest and easiest destination to reach among those around Magma 610. The historic centre is well served by several paid car parks in the areas near Piazza del Duomo and via Etnea, convenient for those arriving by car who then want to explore everything on foot.

## Practical tips

The best time to visit the Pescheria is early morning, before 9-10 am: that's when the market is in full swing, with the freshest goods on display and the most vendors present — by early afternoon the stalls are already starting to close. It's worth planning the Pescheria as the first stop of the day, then continuing toward Piazza del Duomo and via Etnea at a relaxed pace.
