The Alcantara Gorges can be visited practically all year round, but the experience changes noticeably depending on the season you choose. The basalt-rock canyon carved by the Alcantara river, about 40 minutes from Pedara, remains striking at any time of year — what changes is the river's flow, whether you can wade in, and even the general atmosphere of the place. Before planning the trip from Magma 610, it's worth knowing what to expect depending on when you go.

## The best season

For those wanting the fullest experience, wading into the riverbed and perhaps swimming in the natural pools, the period between May and September remains the most suitable: air temperatures make the contrast with the river's cold water enjoyable, which — thanks to its high-altitude source — never gets too warm even in the height of summer.

Those who prefer a more contemplative visit, without necessarily entering the water, might consider autumn or winter: in these months the river's flow increases noticeably compared to summer, giving the canyon a more powerful, dramatic character, although wading in isn't recommended without specific gear (wetsuits, sometimes available for rent). The lower winter sunlight also creates striking contrasts between the dark basalt walls and the green moss and ferns in the damper spots.

## Recommended times

Regardless of the season, early morning remains the best time to visit. During high season — especially July and August — the main car park near Motta Camastra can fill up by mid-morning on weekends, and the walkways leading into the canyon get crowded quickly. Arriving at opening time, or at least in the early morning, lets you enjoy the first stretch of the canyon at a more relaxed pace, before the organised groups start arriving in numbers.

Even in low season, morning remains preferable for the light: the sun reaches the bottom of the canyon only during certain hours of the day, and the early hours generally offer more even lighting for anyone wanting to photograph the basalt walls.

## What to bring

Footwear is probably the most important thing to choose carefully: the rocks in the riverbed are smooth and often slippery, so non-slip footwear with good grip — water shoes or hiking boots with a grooved sole — is preferable to regular sandals. High rubber boots can usually be rented near the main entrance for anyone wanting to wade in without bringing their own gear from home.

A change of clothes is nearly essential for anyone planning to get close to the water, even just for the splashes: packing one in your bag saves you from spending the rest of the day soaked. It's worth remembering that the Alcantara river's water stays cold all year round, thanks to its high-altitude source — a detail that often surprises visitors arriving in high summer expecting warmer water.
