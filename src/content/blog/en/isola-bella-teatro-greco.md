Among all the images that represent Taormina, two come up more often than any other: the scene of the Greek Theatre framing Etna, and the turquoise water of Isola Bella seen from high up on the switchbacks leading down to the coast. These are the city's two iconic sights, and deserve a closer look beyond a simple passing visit.

## The Greek Theatre

Built in the Greek era, probably in the 3rd century BC, and later expanded by the Romans to also host gladiator games, Taormina's Greek Theatre is one of the best-preserved ancient theatres in all of Sicily — and one of the most spectacular for its setting alone. With an estimated capacity of over 5,000 spectators at its peak, it still hosts events and performances during the summer months today, keeping its original function alive after more than two thousand years.

The real reason it's worth visiting, though, goes beyond history: from the stone tiers, the view opens onto one of the most celebrated panoramas in the Mediterranean, with the ancient stage framing both Etna on one side and the Gulf of Naxos on the other. On clear days, especially early in the morning, the view of the volcano from the theatre's upper rows is probably the single most representative shot of a trip to this part of Sicily.

## Isola Bella

At the foot of Taormina, Isola Bella is a rocky islet connected to the mainland by a sandbar that emerges when sea level allows — a phenomenon that earned it the nickname "pearl of the Mediterranean." Since 1998 it has been a protected nature reserve, managed by the WWF, and is home to lush Mediterranean vegetation as well as a small natural history museum inside the villa that once belonged to Lady Florence Trevelyan, the English noblewoman who made the islet famous in the 19th century.

Access to the reserve is regulated, with an entrance fee that contributes to maintaining the protected area. The best time to also enjoy Isola Bella's beach side runs from May to September, when the turquoise water surrounding it invites a swim; in the colder months it remains a striking visit, perhaps quieter and less crowded.

## Tips for the visit

Tickets for the Greek Theatre can be bought directly at the entrance, but in high season it's worth considering buying online in advance to avoid queues, especially during the busier midday hours. To enjoy the theatre at a more relaxed pace and with the best light for photographs, the early hours after opening remain the recommended time.

For Isola Bella, the least crowded time is generally mid-morning on weekdays, before visitors arrive in large numbers during the busier afternoon hours. The descent to the islet can be done on foot along the scenic switchbacks, or with the panoramic cable car departing from the centre of Taormina, a convenient option for those wanting to save their legs for the rest of the day.
