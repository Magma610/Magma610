Taormina is an almost obligatory stop for anyone visiting Sicily's Ionian coast. Perched on a natural terrace between the sea and Etna, it packs into a few hundred metres one of the Mediterranean's most scenic Greek theatres, an elegant old town of stone alleys and boutiques, and one of Sicily's most photographed bays. From Pedara, it's one of the easiest day trips for guests staying at Magma 610.

## What to see

The **Greek Theatre** is the obligatory starting point: built in the Greek era and later expanded by the Romans, it offers one of Italy's most celebrated views, with the ancient stage framing Etna on one side and the sea on the other. It's worth arriving early, both for the best light for photos and to beat the crowds that build up during the middle of the day.

From the theatre, the **old town** is explored almost entirely along Corso Umberto, the main street running through the city from Porta Messina to Porta Catania: baroque churches, medieval palaces, panoramic terraces appearing round every corner, and Piazza IX Aprile, with its open view over the gulf, among the most photographed stops.

Those with time to head down toward the sea will find **Isola Bella**, a small island connected to the coast by a sandbar, reachable by scenic cable car from the centre of Taormina or a longer walk down the switchbacks. It's one of the symbolic images of eastern Sicily, with turquoise water contrasting against Mediterranean vegetation.

## How to get there from Pedara

Taormina is about a 30-35 minute drive from Pedara, mostly on the motorway. It's the most convenient and flexible way to plan the day, especially if you want to combine the visit with other stops in the area, such as the Alcantara Gorges, which lie along a similar route.

Those who'd rather not drive can take the train from Catania's station (Taormina has its own station, from which the old town is reached by a short cable car ride or local bus) and several regional bus lines connecting the Etna area to the city. Both options take longer than driving, but remain valid for those wanting to avoid summer traffic.

## Practical tips

Parking in the centre is limited and often paid: the main car parks (Lumbi and Porta Catania) sit at the edges of town, connected to the old centre by escalators or short shuttle services. In summer, it's worth arriving early in the morning, not just for the best light at the Greek Theatre, but also because traffic toward the centre can slow down arrivals considerably during the middle of the day, especially on high-season weekends.

Corso Umberto is almost entirely pedestrian, so the visit is easily done on foot: just wear comfortable shoes, as several stretches are uphill or on polished stone paving. A full day is generally enough to take in the theatre, the old town and Isola Bella at a relaxed pace, with a lunch break on one of the many panoramic terraces along the way.
