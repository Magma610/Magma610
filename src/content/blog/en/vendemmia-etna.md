Between late September and October, Etna's south side experiences one of the most intense moments of its agricultural calendar: the grape harvest. For guests staying at Magma 610 during this period, it's the perfect chance for a food and wine tour unlike any other — not just tastings, but the opportunity to see the wineries in full swing, with vineyards turning yellow and red and, in some areas, the air filling with the sweet scent of must.

Volcanic soil, mineral-rich and different from one contrada to the next depending on the lava flow that formed it, is what makes Etna wines unique: at this time of year, with the grapes freshly picked, it's easier to understand firsthand why local producers talk about the volcano almost as an extra ingredient, not just a scenic backdrop.

## What happens during the harvest

On Etna, grape picking remains largely done by hand, especially in the older contrade where vineyards grow on hand-built lava-stone terraces dating back generations: the slopes are often too steep and uneven for harvesting machines, and the tradition of hand-picking, crate by crate, remains the prevailing method today for the main grape varieties like Nerello Mascalese.

The exact harvest dates vary from year to year and from contrada to contrada, depending on the weather and the vineyard's altitude — some higher areas may still be harvesting in mid-October, while lower contrade start as early as late August for white varieties like Carricante. It's a detail that makes every visit during this period slightly different from the last: you might find one vineyard already picked and the one next to it still heavy with clusters.

## The wineries worth visiting

The Etna winery scene is varied, and it's worth choosing more than one for a fuller picture. Small family-run wineries, often directly managed for generations, offer a more intimate experience: it's not unusual to be shown around the vines by the producer themselves, with stories ranging from family history to the specific traits of that particular contrada.

Alongside these, more structured operations coexist, some having arrived on Etna over the last twenty years from other Italian wine regions or abroad, offering more organised visit itineraries, often with special attention to communication and welcoming larger groups.

During harvest season, demand for visits rises sharply: many wineries dedicate their staff to picking, and tastings are arranged with fewer available slots than usual. Booking a few days — ideally a few weeks — in advance is almost essential to avoid finding everything full, especially on weekends.

## What to bring and practical tips

Temperatures between late September and October generally stay mild on Etna's south side, but temperature swings between morning and afternoon can be noticeable, especially in the higher contrade: layered clothing is the most practical choice. Comfortable, ideally closed-toe shoes are preferable to sandals — vineyards are often walked on uneven ground, between clods of volcanic soil and narrow rows.

Anyone taking an active part in the harvest at a winery offering this option would do well to bring a change of clothes too, since working among the vines, however brief, gets messy easily. A camera, or just a smartphone, doesn't hurt: the late-September light over the golden vineyards, with Etna in the background, is one of the most photographed sights of the Sicilian autumn.
